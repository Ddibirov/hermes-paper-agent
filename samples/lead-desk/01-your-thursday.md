The Henley review is at half past nine, on a call, and it is the only thing
on the calendar that other people are relying on you for. Expect to be asked
whether the second phase starts in October or waits for the audit; nobody has
answered it since last week. After that the morning is yours until the dentist
at half past two, which is a cleaning and not the crown — that remains
scheduled for the twenty-second, and the reminder to ask after it has moved
to match.

Weather is the other fixed point. Warm and humid by two, cloud building from
the west, and the chance of a storm climbs past even odds a little after five.
If the walk is going to happen it should happen before the dentist, not after.

From your notes, a year ago this week: the fennel came in with the first
frost, and the plan to try again this autumn. Nell arrives Saturday at eleven
by train, into Union Station, and the weekend pages have the case for Great
Falls over the museum while the forecast holds clear.

The rest of the paper is what happened while you slept, filtered for the
parts you have marked as yours, and a few small things from your own notes
that seemed worth seeing again.
