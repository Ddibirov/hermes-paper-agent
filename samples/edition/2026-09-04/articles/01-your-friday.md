The Henley review is at half past nine, on a call, and it is the only thing on
the calendar that other people are relying on you for. The open question is
the phase-two start, and nobody has answered it since last week; expect to be
asked, and expect to be the one who has thought about it most.

After that the morning is yours until the dentist at half past two. It is a
cleaning, not the crown — that reminder has been sitting in your notes for
eight months now, and Tuesday is where it has landed. The surgery is on
Pennsylvania Avenue by Eastern Market, and the walk is the good news of the
day: the Orange Line finished its track work a night early, so the line is
back up and the shuttle buses are being stood down.

Weather is the argument against that walk. Friday is the wettest day of the
week at a 32 per cent chance, and it is still hot — a high of 93°F after
Thursday's 96°F. The rain is not a downpour and the odds are under a third,
but they are the highest odds you will see for seven days, and the afternoon
is when they arrive. If the walk is going to happen it should happen before
half past two, not after.

Then the week turns. Saturday drops to 87°F and Nell arrives at eleven, by
train, into Union Station; Sunday is 83°F, clear, and the Commanders open at
home at one. The forecast holds for both, which is the case for Great Falls
over the museum if the question comes up, and it usually does.

The rest of the paper is what happened while you slept, filtered for the parts
you have marked as yours, and a few small things from your own notes that
seemed worth seeing again.
