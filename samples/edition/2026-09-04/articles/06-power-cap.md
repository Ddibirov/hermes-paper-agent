The grid operator has floated capping how much power new data centres may
draw during peak hours in the region this winter. As proposed, the cap would
pause new connections on the busiest twenty afternoons of the season rather
than throttle anything already running.

Internet providers and the chamber oppose it. The operator's case is that it
is a hedge: twenty afternoons of paused connections against the alternative
of rolling outages, which are not scheduled and do not stop at data centres.

The argument underneath is about who absorbs the cost of a grid that was not
built for this load — the newest customers, or everyone already on it. That
question is not settled by a winter measure, and it will be back.
